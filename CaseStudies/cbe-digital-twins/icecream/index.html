User guide

A live 3D model that resembles an ice cream production line, like the one in UW–Madison's Babcock Hall. Engineering equations set its temperatures, energy and texture; layout, equipment and recipe amounts are illustrative.

How to explore

  • Overview shows the whole plant; every numbered red spot is a ChemE topic.
  • Click a spot or its row in the spots list (left) to zoom in.
  • Drag to orbit, scroll or pinch to zoom.
  • Camera buttons (bottom right): Overview, Mix & HTST, Freezer, Hardening, Ammonia.

Buttons

  • Tour: a guided walk through the plant, one stop at a time.
  • Product design: every ingredient in one gallon, by mass balance.
  • Microstructure: zoom from a pint into ice crystals, air cells and fat.
  • Conditions: live temperatures and flows on the equipment.
  • Temp colours: colours the product and pipes by temperature (blue cold, red hot).
  • Open roof: hides the roof and front walls to see inside.
  • Real world: why the plant matters. Subject buttons: one course's view of the plant.
  • Pause and 1× · 10× · 60×: simulation speed.

Change conditions

  • Side panel: recipe presets and sliders, Process set-points, package.
  • Watch: draw temperature, ice crystals, meltdown, kWh per gallon, cost.
  • ⤢ pops the side panel out into a movable window; ⤡ docks it.

Equations, courses, problems

  • ∑ Model: every equation with its symbols and live values.
  • Links to Courses: course map, and problem sets for each course.
  • Links to Research: UW–Madison CBE research that connects to parts of the plant.

        About this digital twin

        Integrative case study for UW–Madison CBE: balances, thermo, transport, kinetics, control and design in one line.

        Teaching model (ChemE Digital Twins). It resembles a line like Babcock Hall's but is not a model of it: set-points from Babcock Dairy Store's public description; layout, equipment and recipe amounts illustrative. Not built, reviewed or endorsed by Babcock Hall.

        Models, assumptions and sources

        All models run in the page and are simple but physically based. Values marked Typical are representative, not plant data. The line is sized as a small continuous plant (2000 L/h HTST, 500 L/h mix to the freezer, Typical). It is not a model of Babcock's actual equipment, whose sizes are not published.

        • Freezing curve: water activity of sucrose + lactose with Norrish's equation (K = 6.47) equated to the activity of ice, ln aice = 9.69×10⁻³T + 4.76×10⁻⁶T² (Fontan & Chirife). Milk-salt depression is the empirical 2.37·MSNF/W (Goff & Hartel). Unfreezable water is set at Cg′ ≈ 0.8, and Tg′ = −32 °C (Typical).
        • HTST: first-order lethality with D72 = 1.5 s and z = 6.5 °C for L. monocytogenes (Typical), credited in the holding tube at the fastest-particle time. Balanced counter-flow regeneration, ε = 0.85. Legal minima for frozen-dessert mix follow the US PMO (79.4 °C/25 s, 82.2 °C/15 s).
        • Homogeniser: d32 ∝ P−0.6 (Walstra), 0.95 µm at 10 MPa, with a small penalty for high fat. Second stage at 20 % of P₁.
        • Ageing: Avrami crystallisation of milk fat (τ 0.8 h, n 2) toward a Typical solid-fat curve; first-order hydration.
        • Freezer: enthalpy balance on the freezing curve, Harriott penetration theory for the scraped wall, UA → NH₃ evaporating temperature, viscous dissipation for dasher power with a Krieger–Dougherty ice/air viscosity, ideal-gas air for overrun. The ice-crystal size at the draw (≈ 24 µm) and the tempered air-cell scaling are empirical.
        • Hardening: explicit 3-D enthalpy-method conduction in an octant of a pint or 3-gallon tub (round containers approximated by a square section of side 0.85 D), Maxwell–Eucken conductivity, h = 20 W/m² K in the fan-driven deep freezer. Crystals grow with the ice fraction (no new nuclei), then ripen as L³ with WLF kinetics above Tg′, calibrated to 0.042 µm³/s at −18 °C.
        • Refrigeration: ammonia saturation from NIST Antoine constants, latent heat and liquid enthalpy fitted to tables (±1 %), ideal-gas isentropic compression (γ = 1.29), ηs 0.75. Two-stage with an open flash intercooler below −25 °C.
        • Quality: partial coalescence, meltdown and the sensory thresholds are trend models chosen to reproduce known directions and magnitudes (e.g. more fat destabilisation means slower meltdown). Treat their numbers as indicative.
        • Control loop: first-order draw-temperature dynamics (time constant = residence time + 30 s) with a PI controller on NH₃ suction temperature. Mix-flow disturbances ±6 %.

        Course numbers and titles come from the UW–Madison Guide (guide.wisc.edu), checked via search results in Nov 2026; the guide itself could not be fetched directly. CBE 547 is Introduction to Colloid and Interface Science. Food Science courses are labelled as such.

        Process set-points (blend at 110 °F for 15 min, homogenise, pasteurise at ≥ 180 °F for ≥ 15 s, cool below 40 °F, hydrate ≥ 16 h, 80 % overrun, draw ≈ 20 °F, −20 °F deep freezer, pints and 3-gallon tubs) come from the Babcock Dairy Store's public "Ice Cream 101" page. The scene is a generic ice cream plant. Its building loosely resembles public photos of Babcock Hall (UW–Madison News, Grow magazine, 2023), but the layout, equipment and recipe amounts are illustrative, not Babcock's. Room sizes, equipment sizes, capacities, the recipe and the refrigeration plant are not published and are Typical assumptions. It uses no logos and shows no real products. Numbered sources are in References above.
