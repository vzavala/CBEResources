    Why turn seawater into drinking water?

    What is it?

    Pumps press seawater against a membrane that passes water and holds back salt: reverse osmosis.

    Used for

    Drinking water for dry coasts and islands; about half is made in the Middle East and North Africa.

    Why it matters

    Costs: more energy than treating river water; brine to disperse safely; marine life at the intake.

    Where ChemEs fit

    Design, operation, energy cuts, better membranes, brine handling.

      About this digital twin

      Live models (membranes, energy, scaling, control) on a true-scale 3D plant, for the UW–Madison ChemE Digital Twins series. Typical = representative of 60 000 m³/d, not a real plant.

      An integrative case linking your CBE courses.

      Model and assumptions

      One vessel (7 elements × 5 cells), scaled to 6 trains × 112 vessels. Per cell:

      • Osmotic coefficient: Pitzer NaCl form at seawater ionic strength, ×0.974 so \(\varphi = 0.902\) at 35 g/kg, 25 °C (Millero & Leung 1976); ions from reference seawater, scaled with salinity.
      • Membrane (Typical SWRO, 25 °C): \(A\) = 1.1 LMH/bar, \(B\) = 0.05 LMH, boric acid 3.0 LMH; Arrhenius 2640 / 3500 / 5000 K. Ageing: \(A\) → 78 % (3-year time constant), \(B\) +10 %/year. Fouling: reversible loss of \(A\), higher channel \(\Delta p\).
      • Control: PI on permeate flow via HP pump speed (\(K_c\) = 30 bar, \(\tau_I\) = 6 s, pump lag \(\tau_p\) = 6 s); recovery set by feed flow (booster VFD); long steps use the loop's steady state.
      • PX: \(\eta_{\text{PX}} = \sum(pQ)_{\text{out}} / \sum(pQ)_{\text{in}}\), balanced flows, 3 % mixing, 1 % leakage; booster 80 %, motors 96 %.
      • Scaling: Langelier index (brine pH = feed + 0.15, assumed); gypsum from a conditional solubility product, 27 % saturated at \(S = 35\). Ions scale with the concentration factor.
      • Outfall: 60° dense-jet laws (Roberts, Ferrier & Daviero 1997). Post-treatment: first-order approach to 0.5 mmol/L Ca; first-order chlorine decay.
      • Daily swing: ±1.2 °C temperature, ±0.35 g/kg semi-diurnal tide.
      • Layout, sizes and design values: Typical for 60 000 m³/d, not a specific plant.

      User guide

      A true-scale seawater reverse osmosis plant making 60 000 m³ of drinking water a day. Every number comes from live process models you can change.

      How to explore

      • It opens on the Overview: the whole plant, from the offshore intake to the brine outfall.
      • Click a numbered red spot (or its row in the list on the left) to zoom in and open its guide.
      • Drag to orbit, scroll or pinch to zoom, right-drag to pan.
      • Camera buttons at the bottom: Overview, Intake, Pretreatment, RO hall, Post-treatment, Outfall.

      Buttons

      • Equipment tour: step through the main machines with specs, live numbers and equations.
      • Salinity colours: colour pipes and membranes by how salty the water is (blue fresh → red brine).
      • Open buildings: lift roofs and front walls to see inside.
      • Real world: how this plant compares with real ones.
      • Conditions: show live readings over each unit.
      • Balances, Thermo, …: guided lenses, one per chemical engineering subject.
      • ∑ Model: every equation, parameter and assumption.
      • Links to Courses: which UW–Madison CBE courses use this twin, with problem sets.
      • Links to Research: UW–Madison CBE faculty whose research connects to this twin.
      • ⤢ on the right panel: pop it out as a floating window (⤡ docks it).
      • Pause and the rate menu: stop or speed up time.

      Change conditions

      • Operator panel: seawater salinity and temperature, recovery, flux, membrane age and grade, fouling, feed pH, energy recovery, daily swing, cleaning (CIP).
      • Watch Plant (pressure, energy, water quality), the What this means boxes and the red alarm banner.
