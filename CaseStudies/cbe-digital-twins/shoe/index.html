Knee loading by shoe design

Same runner in four shoes, compared with the stability trainer.

Peak force: knee-load proxy. Loading rate: how fast it rises (Eq. 17).

  • Faster loading is linked to stress fractures16,15; peak force is not16.
  • Runners stiffen their legs in maximal shoes14; not modeled here.
  • Softer shoes helped only lighter runners13; no shoe type is proven to prevent injury17.

This is an educational model, not medical or shoe-fitting advice.

From the footstrike model (Eqs. 19–21). Pressures and wear are illustrative; the 90 % threshold is assumed.

  • Real shoes: about 75 % shock absorption after 50 miles, under 60 % by 250–500 miles18.
  • EVA midsoles: peak pressure doubled by 500 km19.

About this digital twin

Integrative CBE case study: one midsole, from gas in a polymer to a marathon.

Gibson–Ashby foam2, loss factor3 calibrated to measured returns1, spring–mass runner4,5, Henry's law and nucleation6. Matches Python to 1e-15.

Generic stability daily trainer: 37/27 mm stack, 10 mm drop, firmer side rails (typical values, assumed). Assumed: stiffness, SC-EVA loss, geometry, gait, foaming; wear illustrative.

User guide

A live digital twin of a running-shoe midsole: a validated model of how the foam is made and how it cushions a runner, shown in 3D. Change the runner, the shoe or the factory and watch the numbers and the leg respond.

How to explore

  • 🏃 It opens on the Overview: the runner's leg and shoe with every numbered spot.
  • 🔴 Click a numbered spot, or its row in the spots list on the left, to zoom in and learn what it is.
  • 🖱️ Drag to orbit, scroll or pinch to zoom; Overview (bottom bar) brings you back.

Buttons and views

  • Real · the shoe in its real colours.
  • Squash · colours the foam by how hard it is compressed.
  • Pressure · heat map of the pressure under the foot, and the load path up the shin to a glowing knee.
  • Inside the sole · cuts the shoe open to show the foam.
  • Foam bubbles · the gas cells inside the foam, about 50 µm each.
  • Polymer chains · the hard and soft blocks that make the foam springy.
  • Foam factory · the autoclave where gas puffs plastic into foam.
  • Pause / Speed · stop the stride or change slow motion (1/8 by default).
  • Real world · why shoes are a ChemE story. Learn by subject · a guided stop per course topic.
  • ⤢ (top of the side panel) · pop the panel out as a movable window; ⤡ docks it.

Change conditions, watch the results

  • Conditions opens Your run (weight, pace, surface), The shoe (foam, density, plate, distance) and Foam factory (gas, pressure, temperature).
  • Watch Energy return, Peak force and the force–squash loop; the knee and distance sections compare shoes and runs.
