User guide

A live model of a football game day. Every number comes from chemical-engineering equations running in your browser.

How to explore
  • Overview (camera bar): the whole stadium with all 10 numbered ChemE spots.
  • Click a spot, or its row in the spots list, to zoom in and open its card.
  • Drag to orbit · scroll or pinch to zoom · camera bar for set views.
Buttons and views
  • Thermal view: colours the field by its modelled surface temperature, shadows included.
  • Engineering tour: ten stops with specs, equations and 3D cutaways.
  • Real world: why game-day chemical engineering matters.
  • Conditions: building names and the pass trajectory on or off.
  • Subject buttons (Balances, Thermo, …): guided cards, one per course subject.
  • Play / Rate (bottom): run the game clock and set its speed.
  • ⤢ (side panel): pop the panel out as a floating window; ⤡ docks it.
Change conditions
  • Weather, Field, Ball and pass, Gear, Lineman (side panel): pick a preset or set values.
  • Watch the live charts under the 3D view (turf, ball, lineman), the Gameday readouts and the red What this means boxes.
Equations, courses, problems
  • ∑ Model: every equation, parameter, method and check.
  • Links to Courses: which CBE courses use what, with problem sets.
  • Links to Research: UW–Madison CBE research that connects to the twin.
  • References and About: bottom of the side panel.

        About this digital twin

        Model, assumptions and sources

        An integrative case study for UW–Madison CBE students: one game day, many courses. Every number is computed live. Typical = literature range, Standard = rulebook or test standard, Assumed = our choice. Full detail in ∑ Model.

        • Weather and sun. Madison (43.07° N, 89.41° W, 98.3 kPa). Solar position from declination, hour angle and equation of time; clear-sky beam from the Meinel air-mass formula, clouds by Kasten–Czeplak; sky long-wave by Brutsaert. Air temperature follows a cosine from the 3 am low to the 3 pm high (10 °C swing, less when cloudy); vapour pressure is fixed by the afternoon humidity.
        • Turf. One-node surface energy balance integrated over two days (periodic state). Albedo 0.08 / 0.16 / 0.23, heat capacity 30 / 34 / 60 kJ/m²K, ground coupling 3–10 W/m²K, grass canopy resistance 70 s/m. Stand shadows are ray-marched against the stand geometry.
        • G-max. ASTM F355-A missile on a single compressible layer: , stiffening 16 %/year with infill compaction and 1.2 %/°C with cold. Calibrated to typical new-field values near 100–120 g.
        • Ball. Lemon-of-revolution geometry fitted to the short circumference and length (V ≈ 3.7 L). Ideal gas at fixed volume; lumped thermal mass 0.41 kg × 1.5 kJ/kg·K; O₂ and N₂ permeation through butyl or latex with handbook permeabilities and an Arrhenius temperature factor. Valve leaks are not modelled.
        • Pass. 3D point mass with drag , the gyroscopic yaw of repose , a side force and a Magnus term, integrated with RK4. Aerodynamic coefficients are approximate.
        • Helmet. 4.9 kg headform on a crushable, rate- and temperature-dependent foam with a growing contact patch and a polycarbonate shell spring in series. Gives about 95 g at 5.5 m/s and 175–210 g at 9.3 m/s, in the range of published lab tests. Rotational acceleration, which matters for concussion, is not modelled.
        • Player. ISO 7933-style heat balance with clothing insulation and evaporative resistance reduced 40 % for movement, Lewis relation , sweating as a proportional controller on core temperature, Edelman's plasma-sodium relation. Not for medical or safety decisions.
        • Drinks. Ideal osmolality from the composition; gastric emptying first order for water and energy-limited for carbohydrate drinks (illustrative).
        • Field heating. ASHRAE snow-melt load, conduction shape factor for a row of buried tubes, glycol energy balance, boiler at 90 %.
