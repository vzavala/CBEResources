      User guide

      A live model of Lake Mendota's ice through a real Madison winter. It shows how heat flow sets the ice thickness, when the ice is safe, and what a warmer climate does.

      How to explore

      • Starts on the Overview: every numbered spot is visible.
      • Click a numbered spot, or its row in the spot list, to zoom in and learn.
      • Drag to orbit · scroll / pinch to zoom · right-drag to pan.
      • Camera buttons: Overview, Ice edge, Shanties (Olaf, Bob, Warren and Ed up close), Cutaway.

      Buttons

      • Play / Pause, Speed, day slider: run or scrub the winter.
      • Real ice / Temperature: the cut face shows real ice with bubbles, or its temperature (purple cold → white 0 °C).
      • Real world: why lake ice matters. Subject chips (Balances … Design): guided tours.
      • Conditions: today's numbers over the 3D view, and the weather controls.
      • ⤢ on the side panel: pop it out as a floating window; ⤡ docks it back.

      Change the conditions

      • Winter & weather: any real winter since 1948, or a constant "Textbook cold".
      • Warmer climate slider: adds 0–4 °C to every day and shifts freeze-up later.
      • Snow and wind: snow insulates; wind speeds freezing.
      • Watch the thickness, the status chip, the season chart and the climate charts.

      Go deeper

      • ∑ Model: every equation, symbol, parameter and check.
      • Links to Courses: course map and problem sets. References: bottom of the side panel.
      • Links to Research: UW–Madison research that connects to this twin.
