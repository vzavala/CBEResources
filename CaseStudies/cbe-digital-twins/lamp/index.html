        User guide

        A lava-lamp style lamp as an integrative case study: an energy balance, buoyancy and drag, liquid–liquid equilibrium and coalescence kinetics in one system you can test against a real lamp.

        Disclaimer: This is for educational purposes only. This digital twin is based on a combination of public data, scientific publications and fictionalized settings.

        How to explore

        • Blob view (on at the start; press it to hide): a zoomed view of one blob beside the lamp, with its temperature, density and the forces on it. A red frame marks that blob on the lamp; Next blob picks another.
        • Press Lab spots to show the numbered spots on the lamp.
        • Click a numbered spot, or its row in the spot list, to zoom in and learn.
        • Drag to orbit · scroll / pinch to zoom · right-drag to pan.
        • Camera buttons: Overview, Lamp, Base, Top, Desk.

        Play with the lamp

        • °C | °F: switch the temperature unit at the top of the panel. It changes what you see, not the model.
        • Bulb power and Room temperature: the two things that decide if it flows.
        • Wax and Liquid colours: pick your own.
        • Start from cold: watch the warm-up. Jump to warmed up: skip it.
        • Shake it!: the wax breaks into thousands of tiny droplets that merge again. Only in the twin: never shake a real warm lamp.
        • Speed: lab time per second. Blobs are easiest to follow at 1 s = 10 s.
        • Conditions map: click any square to try those conditions.
        • Investigate: questions to answer with the controls.

        Check it against a real lamp

        • My real lamp: enter the bulb, the room temperature and your two timings.
        • Tune the twin to my warm-up, then see if it predicts your blob time.

        Make and break two layers

        • Phase lab → Kitchen: salt splits rubbing alcohol and water into two layers; water joins them again.
        • Phase lab → School lab: water, ethanol and castor oil, with the full equilibrium calculation.
        • Properties of the two fluids: change how much each liquid swells when heated, and the drag on a blob.

        Buttons

        • Normal / Thermal: thermal shows temperature as colour (dark = cool, bright = hot), inside the lamp too.
        • Real world and topic chips: short guided tours. Conditions: live numbers over the 3D view.
        • ∑ Model: every equation, symbol, parameter, limit and check. Links to Courses: course map and problem sets. Links to Research: related research areas in the department. References: bottom of the side panel.
        • ⤢ on the side panel: pop it out as a floating window; ⤡ docks it back.

        Safety with a real lamp

        • The base and glass get very hot: look, time, do not touch or move.
        • Never shake a warm lamp, and never heat one with anything but its own bulb.
